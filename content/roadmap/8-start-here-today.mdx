## Do these five things now

[Start here](/roadmap/start-here) explains what this site is and
[the plan](/roadmap/3-the-integrated-plan-everything-sequenced-your-chart)
explains the fifteen weeks. This page is the next two and a half hours. Work
through it in order; none of it requires you to have read anything else first.

### 1. Fix the committed credentials — 30 minutes

Open `application.yml` in the URL shortener. It contains a hardcoded JWT signing
secret and hardcoded database credentials.

Move both to environment variables or a secrets manager. Make the application
fail loudly at startup if they are absent rather than falling back to a default —
a default secret is worse than no secret, because it ships silently. Rotate the
values that were committed, since anything in git history should be treated as
public. Add a `.env.example` listing what is required.

**This is first because it is the one item on the list with a deadline that is
not yours.** You are about to start linking this repository on a resume, and a
committed signing key is the first thing a senior reviewer notices. It turns a
decent project into a conversation about your security judgement.

### 2. Run the DSA diagnostic — 50 minutes

Pick two patterns from [the DSA track](/dsa) that you have not touched recently —
[dynamic programming](/dsa/pattern-12-dynamic-programming) and
[graphs](/dsa/pattern-11-graphs-bfs-dfs-union-find) are the usual candidates.
Solve one medium in each, with a twenty-five-minute timer, **talking out loud
the entire time.**

The point is not the problems. It is to find out two things: how rusty you
actually are, and how it feels to narrate while thinking. If the narration
feels absurd and difficult, that is the most useful information you will get
today, because it is the skill the coding round measures and the one that
silent practice never builds.

Write down which patterns felt slow. That list is your secondary track for the
next three weeks.

### 3. Read the system design framework — 25 minutes

Read [the framework](/system-design/1-the-framework-how-to-drive-a-45-minute-design-interview)
once, straight through.

Do not try to learn it. The purpose today is to see the shape of the thing you
are going to spend six of the next fifteen weeks on, and to notice that the
round is scored on process rather than on knowing an answer. That reframing is
most of what makes the phase survivable, and you want it in your head from day
one rather than from week five.

While you read, note how much of it is already in your project: cache-aside
reads, a rate limiter, a database behind a cache, an analytics write path. You
are not starting from zero on this track. You are starting from having built a
small version of the canonical question without knowing that is what it was.

### 4. Rewrite the project bullets on your resume — 40 minutes

Open [resume guidance](/roadmap/6-resume-guidance-projects-skills-sections) and
rewrite the URL shortener section using the action-technology-outcome shape.
Four or five bullets.

Then do the part that actually matters: for each bullet, write down the three
follow-up questions an interviewer would ask, and answer them in writing. Any
bullet whose follow-ups you cannot answer gets deleted or gets fixed. **Every
line on a resume is forty-five minutes of interview surface, and you get to
choose what you are asked about.**

Do this today rather than later because applications have a four-to-six-week
delay built into them, and the resume is the only item in the whole plan that
is on someone else's clock.

### 5. Set up the tracker and book tomorrow — 15 minutes

Make a spreadsheet with one row per week and these columns: phase, sessions with
output, out-loud tests passed, timed attempts, notes. See
[progress tracking](/roadmap/5-progress-tracking) for why those four and not
hours.

Then put two blocks in your calendar for tomorrow — forty-five minutes in the
morning and ninety in the evening — and treat them as meetings.
[Your weekly rhythm](/roadmap/4-your-weekly-rhythm-the-shape-of-a-day) explains
the shape. The calendar entry is doing real work here: the plan fails far more
often from never starting the evening block than from studying the wrong thing
in it.

## This week

With today done, the rest of week 0 is short:

- Read [how product-company hiring actually works](/roadmap/1-how-product-company-hiring-actually-works)
  and [the four tracks](/roadmap/2-the-four-tracks). About forty minutes, and
  they explain why the plan is weighted the way it is.
- Finish the rest of the resume and send it to **five companies you do not
  especially want** — you need the pipeline running and you need the first loop
  to be a cheap one.
- Skim the remaining DSA pattern lessons and mark the weak ones.

Then start [phase 1](/roadmap/3-the-integrated-plan-everything-sequenced-your-chart)
on Monday.

## What not to do today

**Do not start learning system design properly.** It is the biggest track and
starting it at the end of a long day, out of sequence, produces two hours of
reading you will not retain. It has six weeks allocated. Let it have them.

**Do not buy anything.** The books in
[where to actually solve problems](/roadmap/7-where-to-actually-solve-problems)
have a week or more before you need them, and buying resources is a very
effective substitute for starting.

**Do not redesign the plan.** Adjusting a study plan is enjoyable, feels
productive, and produces nothing. The plan is adequate. The variable that
decides the outcome is whether you sit down tomorrow evening.
