## How to use this

Read the question, **answer it out loud, in full sentences, before looking at
the grading note.** That instruction is not a formality — it's the entire point.

The gap between "I know this" and "I can say this" is where interviews are
lost. Recognising an answer is effortless and feels identical to knowing it,
which is why silent revision produces confident candidates who freeze when
asked. Speaking forces you to produce the words in order, discover the sentence
you can't finish, and hear yourself trail off. That failure is the information
you came for.

Each question below is followed by **the one thing a good answer must
contain.** If your spoken answer didn't contain it, mark the question and go
back to the lesson. Don't award yourself partial credit for "I knew that" — you
either said it or you didn't.

<Callout type="tip" title="Grade on the follow-up, not the first answer">
Almost everyone can produce the first sentence. The interview happens in the
second and third. So for each question, after you answer, ask yourself **"and
why?"** twice more. If the second "why" produces silence, you have a bookmark
rather than an understanding — which is exactly the failure this whole site is
built to fix.
</Callout>

## Networking

1. **Walk me through what happens when I type a URL and press Enter.**
   *Must contain:* the seven stages, and that DNS, TCP and TLS are roughly three
   round trips of pure latency before the server sees a byte.
2. **Why is the first request to a server slower than the second?**
   *Must contain:* handshakes, plus TCP slow start ramping the congestion
   window.
3. **TCP vs UDP — a real use case for each.**
   *Must contain:* that TCP's ordering guarantee is actively harmful for live
   media, where a stale retransmitted frame is worse than a dropped one.
4. **What does the TCP handshake accomplish?**
   *Must contain:* agreeing initial sequence numbers and confirming both
   directions, at a cost of one round trip.
5. **What is `TIME_WAIT` and why should you care?**
   *Must contain:* ephemeral port exhaustion, and that the fix is connection
   pooling.
6. **Did HTTP/2 solve head-of-line blocking?**
   *Must contain:* only at the application layer — it moved the problem into
   TCP, where one lost packet stalls every stream.
7. **Why does HTTP/3 run over UDP?**
   *Must contain:* per-stream reliability in QUIC, plus a merged transport and
   TLS handshake.
8. **Which HTTP methods are idempotent, and why does it matter?**
   *Must contain:* that idempotency is what makes a retry safe.
9. **`301` vs `302`, `401` vs `403`, `502` vs `503` vs `504`.**
   *Must contain:* 401 is unauthenticated despite its name; 504 specifically
   means the upstream timed out.
10. **What does TLS actually give you?**
    *Must contain:* three properties — confidentiality, integrity **and**
    authentication — and asymmetric to agree a symmetric key.
11. **Why not use asymmetric encryption for everything?**
    *Must contain:* orders of magnitude slower.
12. **When would you choose gRPC over REST?**
    *Must contain:* service-to-service, and that REST wins at the browser edge.

## Operating systems and concurrency

13. **Process vs thread, and when does a context switch happen?**
    *Must contain:* isolation versus cost, and that the indirect cost of a
    switch (cold caches) usually exceeds the direct cost.
14. **Why doesn't adding threads add throughput indefinitely?**
    *Must contain:* switching overhead and cache thrash grow while cores don't.
15. **How would you size a thread pool?**
    *Must contain:* about the core count for CPU-bound, much higher for
    I/O-bound, scaled by the wait-to-compute ratio.
16. **What are the four conditions for deadlock, and how do you prevent one?**
    *Must contain:* all four named, and that you only need to break one — then
    say which one your fix breaks.
17. **Deadlock vs livelock vs starvation.**
    *Must contain:* stuck and idle; busy but not progressing; never scheduled.
18. **Mutex vs semaphore.**
    *Must contain:* ownership — a mutex must be released by its locker.
19. **When is a spinlock the right choice?**
    *Must contain:* when the expected wait is shorter than a context switch, and
    never on a single core.
20. **What is the C10K problem and how was it solved?**
    *Must contain:* thread-per-connection memory and scheduler cost; event loops
    with `epoll`, and now virtual threads.
21. **`select` vs `epoll`.**
    *Must contain:* O(n) rescan per call versus register-once and get only what
    is ready.
22. **What do virtual threads change?**
    *Must contain:* the JVM unmounts a blocked virtual thread from its carrier,
    so blocking code scales — unless it pins.
23. **What is thrashing?**
    *Must contain:* the working set exceeding RAM, so the machine pages instead
    of computing.
24. **Why is a system call expensive?**
    *Must contain:* the trap into the kernel, which is why I/O is buffered.

## Java and the JVM

25. **Why does the JVM have generations?**
    *Must contain:* the weak generational hypothesis, and that copying cost
    scales with survivors rather than allocations.
26. **Which garbage collector, and why?**
    *Must contain:* G1 by default; ZGC when tail latency beats 10–15% of
    throughput.
27. **How does ZGC get sub-millisecond pauses?**
    *Must contain:* coloured pointers and a load barrier, so relocation is
    concurrent.
28. **You set `-Xmx` to the container's memory limit. What happens?**
    *Must contain:* stacks, Metaspace, code cache and direct buffers are outside
    `-Xmx`, so the kernel kills the process with no Java error.
29. **You get an `OutOfMemoryError`. Walk me through what you do.**
    *Must contain:* take a heap dump, analyse the dominator tree, and
    distinguish a leak from an undersized heap by old-gen occupancy after full
    GCs.
30. **How does `HashMap` work?**
    *Must contain:* hash spreading, the power-of-two mask, chaining,
    treeification and resize at 75%.
31. **Why does `HashMap` treeify at 8?**
    *Must contain:* it's a defence against deliberate hash collisions, not an
    optimisation for normal use.
32. **What breaks if you override `equals` but not `hashCode`?**
    *Must contain:* equal objects land in different buckets, so `get` returns
    null for a key that's present.
33. **Is `HashMap` thread-safe since Java 8?**
    *Must contain:* no — Java 8 removed the resize infinite loop, not the
    unsafety.
34. **Why does `ConcurrentHashMap` forbid null?**
    *Must contain:* a null return would be ambiguous and you can't disambiguate
    it without a race.
35. **`ArrayList` or `LinkedList`?**
    *Must contain:* ArrayList, because cache locality beats the Big-O — and
    `ArrayDeque` for queues.
36. **When is a parallel stream a bad idea?**
    *Must contain:* it shares the JVM-wide common ForkJoinPool, so blocking I/O
    inside one starves everything else.
37. **`orElse` vs `orElseGet`.**
    *Must contain:* `orElse`'s argument is evaluated even when a value is
    present.
38. **Why is type erasure the reason you can't do `new T[]`?**
    *Must contain:* generics are compile-time only; there is no `T` at runtime.

## Spring and data access

39. **Why does calling a `@Transactional` method from within the same class do
    nothing?**
    *Must contain:* self-invocation bypasses the proxy.
40. **Does a checked exception roll back a transaction?**
    *Must contain:* no, by default — only unchecked ones do.
41. **What is the N+1 problem and how do you detect it?**
    *Must contain:* one query per lazily-loaded association, and that you detect
    it by counting queries, not by reading code.
42. **`JOIN FETCH` with pagination — what happens?**
    *Must contain:* Hibernate pages in memory after loading everything.
43. **First-level vs second-level cache.**
    *Must contain:* L1 is the per-transaction persistence context and can't be
    disabled; L2 is shared and only safe when your app is the sole writer.
44. **Why is `OFFSET` pagination slow?**
    *Must contain:* the database produces and discards every skipped row.

## Databases and SQL

45. **What's the default isolation level, and what can still go wrong?**
    *Must contain:* Read Committed in Postgres, and that lost updates are still
    possible.
46. **Explain MVCC in one breath.**
    *Must contain:* readers don't block writers because each row has versions
    and a snapshot decides visibility.
47. **A user updates their profile and doesn't see the change. Why?**
    *Must contain:* replication lag, and read-your-own-writes as the fix.
48. **Sync or async replication?**
    *Must contain:* async is the default and loses recent writes on failover.
49. **What is split-brain and how do you prevent it?**
    *Must contain:* two nodes both accepting writes after a bad failover;
    quorum decisions and fencing.
50. **How would you shard, and what breaks?**
    *Must contain:* never `hash % N`; and that joins, cross-shard transactions
    and auto-increment IDs all break.
51. **Why is an index on a low-cardinality column often useless?**
    *Must contain:* past a selectivity threshold a sequential scan beats random
    I/O, and the planner knows it.
52. **What kills an index silently?**
    *Must contain:* a function on the column, implicit type coercion, or a
    leading wildcard.

## Security

53. **How do you store passwords, and why not SHA-256?**
    *Must contain:* bcrypt or Argon2 because password hashing must be
    deliberately slow; SHA-256 is billions per second on a GPU.
54. **What does a salt prevent?**
    *Must contain:* rainbow tables and cracking every account at once — not
    making one password harder.
55. **Is a JWT encrypted?**
    *Must contain:* no, signed — anyone can read the payload.
56. **How do you log a user out with JWTs?**
    *Must contain:* you can't really; short-lived access tokens plus a revocable
    refresh token.
57. **How do you prevent SQL injection?**
    *Must contain:* parameterised queries as separate channels for code and
    data — not escaping.
58. **Do you need CSRF protection on a token API?**
    *Must contain:* not if the token is in an `Authorization` header; yes the
    moment it lives in a cookie.
59. **Is CORS a security control?**
    *Must contain:* no — it governs which origins may read a response and never
    replaces server-side authorization.

## Distributed systems and design

60. **What makes distributed systems hard?**
    *Must contain:* you cannot distinguish a slow node from a dead one, or a
    lost request from a lost reply.
61. **How do you make a write safe to retry?**
    *Must contain:* an idempotency key recorded atomically with the effect.
62. **What does `W + R > N` buy you?**
    *Must contain:* overlap between the read and write sets.
63. **2PC or saga?**
    *Must contain:* 2PC blocks when the coordinator dies; a saga trades away
    isolation for availability.
64. **Why can't you order distributed events by timestamp?**
    *Must contain:* clock skew, and that NTP can move a clock backwards.
65. **What's wrong with a Redis distributed lock?**
    *Must contain:* a stalled client holding a stale belief past the TTL;
    fencing tokens are the fix.
66. **Why exponential backoff *with jitter*?**
    *Must contain:* without jitter, every client retries in lockstep and
    re-breaks the recovering dependency.
67. **What's a cache stampede and how do you fix it?**
    *Must contain:* a hot key expiring and every request missing at once;
    coalesce, refresh early, or don't expire hot keys.
68. **How would you know a queue-based system was unhealthy?**
    *Must contain:* consumer lag and message age — error rate stays green while
    everything is merely slow.

## Scoring yourself honestly

| You got | What it means |
|---|---|
| Under 40 | Breadth gaps. Work through the lessons rather than re-testing. |
| 40–55 | Solid breadth. Now drill the ones you missed and re-test in a week. |
| 55–65 | Interview-ready on fundamentals. Spend your time on system design and coding. |
| All 68, with two "why?" follow-ups each | You are past what this test can measure. |

The number matters less than the pattern. **Three misses clustered in one
section is a lesson to reread; three scattered misses are just revision.** And
re-test a week later rather than immediately — being able to answer a question
you just read is not evidence of anything.

<Callout type="interview" title="The three questions that carry the most weight">
If you only rehearse three, make them these, because they're the ones that most
reliably open into a long, well-scored conversation:

1. **"Walk me through what happens when I type a URL and press Enter."** It
   touches DNS, TCP, TLS, HTTP, load balancing, caching and rendering, so the
   interviewer can go anywhere from it.
2. **"How would you scale this?"** The answer that scores is finding the thing
   that can't be duplicated, not saying "horizontally."
3. **"How would you know it was working?"** Consistently under-prepared, and it
   separates people who have operated a system from people who have drawn one.
</Callout>
